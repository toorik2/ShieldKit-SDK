# RFC-0002: qualify the seven-input verifier against actual BCH limits

Status: accepted for `g0-v3`

Date: 2026-07-23

## Problem

The `g0-v2` kill gate imposed two project-local cushions in addition to BCH's
actual limits:

- a 9,500-byte ceiling on each unlocking bytecode; and
- at least five percent measured headroom in every applicable
  operation-density and stack budget.

Those cushions are not BCH consensus or standardness requirements. They also
create pressure to repartition a verifier that already executes successfully
within the actual BCH limits.

The independently reproduced verifier.cash candidate
`bn254-onetx-pf7-sub62-r1` at commit
`26468ae29004d2401619032de2a6ec8de269a4d6` uses seven verifier inputs and a
54,296-byte serialized reference transaction. Its largest unlocking bytecode is
9,176 bytes. It is the intended optimized topology; the approximately
82-kilobyte generic verifier-bundle adapter is not an acceptable substitute.

## Old decision

- Each unlocking bytecode had to be at most 9,500 bytes.
- Every applicable operation-density and stack budget had to retain at least
  five percent measured headroom.
- Proof-system selection remained open among measured candidates.

## Decision

Qualify the exact seven-input `bn254-onetx-pf7-sub62-r1` topology against actual
BCH consensus and standardness limits:

- each unlocking bytecode must be at most 10,000 bytes;
- every applicable operation, density, stack, transaction-size, and
  standardness check must pass;
- no additional percentage headroom is required;
- actual resource use and remaining margin must still be measured and reported;
- verifier partitioning, padding, or a larger generic wrapper must not be used
  to manufacture margin; and
- the selected complete transaction target is the 54-to-59-kilobyte class, with
  59,000 serialized bytes as the G2 ceiling for this profile.

The existing 54,739-byte figure remains the initial fee-calculation baseline,
not a hard complete-transaction ceiling.

The verifier remains profile-bound under RFC-0001. Parameterizing the
setup-dependent constants must preserve the selected seven-input topology.
Failure to reproduce a locally initialized profile within this envelope is a
candidate failure, not permission to fall back to the approximately
82-kilobyte topology.

## Consequences

- The proven 54,296-byte, seven-input verifier can be used as-is.
- The protocol checks deterministic BCH limits rather than an invented safety
  percentage.
- Ceremony-derived or development setup constants may vary, but their generated
  verifier must retain the same partition and measured size class.
- An operation-margin figure is evidence, not an acceptance criterion.
- Complete settlement transactions are measured with all state, fee, change,
  and action fields and rejected above 59,000 serialized bytes.

## Invalidated gates and evidence

- G0 is reopened to replace the locked margin policy.
- G1 is reset while the seven-input verifier is reproduced from the typed
  development verifier bundles.
- G1 conclusions that rejected an otherwise valid candidate solely because it
  crossed the 9,500-byte project ceiling or lacked five percent project-local
  headroom are invalidated.
- Raw measurements, proof-verification results, adversarial results, BCH VM
  verdicts, and setup artifacts remain evidence. They are not rewritten or
  deleted.
